Alright, that was news item number one. Now let’s shift our eyes to the future, because the trade deadline is right here, and there’s movement on multiple fronts.

We already made the huge swing by trading for Jeffery Simmons. That was the blockbuster. But now it’s time to focus on the cornerback position. We’ve been talking about Riq Woolen as a possibility, and that conversation isn’t over. But there’s another name heating up, and it’s one Bears fans know very well.

The New York Jets are now 2 and 7. Before this weekend’s game, the word was that the Jets weren’t ready to trade anyone. But Josina Anderson provided an update, and according to her, the Bears have been confirmed to be talking to the Jets about Nahshon Wright.

You know who Nahshon Wright is. His familiarity with this team is obvious. He was here last season, and he was a ballhawk. Five interceptions last year. Five. And what has New York done with him? They’ve barely used him. He’s there on a one-year prove-it deal, and the Jets are proving, game after game, that they simply don’t have a spot for him in their defense.

So let’s think about this from New York’s side. The Jets are not in the middle of a playoff push. They’re 2 and 7. Their season is heading in one direction, and it’s not toward January. There is absolutely no reason for them to cling to a player they’re not even using. And from Chicago’s side, that means the Bears wouldn’t have to overpay. This isn’t a bidding war. This is a team with a player it doesn’t need, and another team that knows exactly what that player can do.

I think this is all about finding depth and flexibility at cornerback. And Wright brings something you can’t teach in a midseason trade: he already knows the defense. He knows the coaches. He knows the terminology. He knows his teammates. There’s no learning curve. He can walk into the building and be ready to contribute almost immediately.

Now, let’s be real about who Nahshon Wright is. He’s a boom-or-bust player. We’ve said it before. He gives up some plays. But he also makes plays. He has a real knack for turning the ball over, and on a defense that just proved it can win games by taking the football away, that’s exactly the kind of player you want in your rotation. If you’re going to have a corner who takes risks, you want one whose risks pay off with interceptions. And Wright’s history says they do.

I think this is a perfect fit. Ryan Poles can make the easy move with Wright. It’s cheap, it’s low-risk, and it addresses a need. And at the same time, he can continue negotiating with Philadelphia about Riq Woolen. These two moves aren’t mutually exclusive. You can bring back Wright for depth and still chase a bigger upgrade if the price is right. I’m telling you, I think we’re definitely going to see a cornerback move. And I absolutely think that’s the move this team should make.

Alright, and now for the third piece of great news, and this one kind of got lost in the shuffle this weekend. The New Orleans Saints made a move I did not see coming. They released running back Kendre Miller. And this was a player I genuinely did not expect to be available. Honestly, I think it’s a mistake by the Saints to cut him. But their mistake could be Chicago’s gain.

Let’s talk about why this matters for the Bears. First, the good news on the running back front. Kyle Monangai should be back after the bye. And that’s super welcome news. The guy is a great player. He can carry the load by himself if D’Andre Swift gets banged up. And speaking of the backfield, I think we saw a little blip against Tampa where things looked like they had some pop. But Kendre Miller is a different class of player.

The cupboard at running back looks pretty bare right now, with injuries piling up in that room. And Roschon Johnson, look, I respect the guy. He’s worked hard. He’s done everything asked of him. But I just don’t think he’s the answer.

And that’s what makes this so interesting. Because according to reports, the Bears are the favorites to sign the newly released Kendre Miller to the practice squad. He was released by the Saints on Saturday, and let me tell you, I think this is more than a minor surprise. I think it’s significant.

Here’s why. Miller was playing starter’s reps for New Orleans. Travis Etienne has been hurt for quite some time, and Kendre Miller was really a guy the Saints were depending on. This is a former third-round pick who spent his entire career with the Saints. He’s shown real flashes of capability throughout his time in New Orleans. But he never got full workloads. Alvin Kamara was there. Etienne was there. He was always stuck behind somebody, always waiting for his turn, always fighting for scraps.

And that’s why I think he’s the perfect insurance policy. If one of the Bears’ running backs gets hurt down the stretch, and in November and December that’s always a real possibility, Miller is a guy who can step in and contribute. In fact, I think he should be activated ahead of Roschon Johnson. You know what, Roschon has played some nice snaps, I’ll give him that. But I’m still not impressed with him compared to what Miller brings to the table.

Let’s look at the résumé. Go back to his college days. As a junior in 2022, Kendre Miller had 17 touchdowns. Seventeen. And he piled up around 1,500 total yards when you add in his receiving numbers. That’s production. That’s a workhorse.

And coming into the draft, he was touted as a player with really good size, really good vision, and the ability to break tackles. That’s exactly the kind of back the Bears love. A guy who finds the holes and takes the yardage that’s there. He doesn’t dance. He doesn’t waste time. He sees it, he hits it, and he falls forward.

Now, to be fair, the knock on him during the draft process was that he wasn’t a home-run hitter. He’s not the guy who’s going to rip off 80-yard touchdowns every other week. And in New Orleans, he spent a couple of years in the doghouse. The Saints just would not play him consistently. But here’s what stands out. Miller has a reputation for taking coaching really, really well. And in Chicago, he’d be under the guidance of Ben Johnson, one of the sharpest offensive minds in football, along with one of the best running back coaches in the entire NFL. You put that kind of coaching together with a real need at running back, and I think this is an absolutely perfect fit.

And here’s the best part. It won’t cost the Bears any draft capital. None. After giving up two first-round picks for Simmons, that matters. Every free addition that makes this roster better is a win for Ryan Poles.

There’s another connection here, too. Dennis Allen was with the Saints when they drafted Miller. So the Bears’ defensive coordinator knows exactly who this guy is as a person and as a worker. And the word on Miller back then was simple: good work ethic, great coachability. Those are the two traits you want in a player coming into a new building midseason.

And here’s a bonus. Miller also has experience returning kicks on special teams. So even if he’s not getting carries right away, he can contribute in the third phase of the game. That kind of versatility is gold for a team trying to make a deep playoff run.

I really think this change of scenery would be perfect for him. Sometimes a player just needs a fresh start, a new coaching staff, and a team that actually believes in him. Chicago could be exactly that place.

So let me put it all together. I would love both of these players coming in. Bring in Nahshon Wright for depth at cornerback. He’s a perfect plug-and-play guy who can hit the ground running because he already knows this defense, and he’s got a proven knack for turning the ball over. Then make a quiet, sneaky move and add Kendre Miller to the running back room. No draft picks, no big contract, just smart roster building. I think those are the two perfect fits for this team right now.

The insurance policies. These aren’t the moves that lead SportsCenter. But they’re often the moves that decide whether a good team survives the grind of December or falls apart because of injuries.

Think about how each of these names fits that idea. Marshon Lattimore is cheap insurance at a position that’s been unstable all year, with a coordinator who knows him better than anyone. Taylor Decker is an emergency plan at left tackle, with years of history in Ben Johnson’s offense. Joey Bosa is a break-glass-in-case-of-emergency option if the edge group gets hit by injuries. Will Dissly is a potential upgrade in the heavy packages that matter more as the weather turns. And Jordan Elliott is the most complicated one of all, a talented run stopper with a serious red flag that the front office has to weigh carefully.

None of them is a superstar anymore. Some of them may never sign anywhere. But the best front offices don’t wait for injuries to happen and then scramble. They keep a list. They do their homework. They make the calls before they need to. And based on that workout yesterday, it looks like Ryan Poles is doing exactly that.

And remember, the bye week is the perfect time for this kind of work. The players are resting. The coaches are reviewing tape. The front office has a little breathing room to evaluate options. Whatever moves come next, they’ll be made with a clear picture of who’s healthy, who’s not, and where the real weak spots are.

Let’s recap everything. The trade deadline has passed, and Jeffery Simmons was the only big move, which means holes remain at outside corner, edge, tight end, defensive tackle, and left tackle. Marshon Lattimore, the 2017 Defensive Rookie of the Year with eight seasons under Dennis Allen in New Orleans, allowed a 93 passer rating with seven pass breakups and an interception in nine games last season, and he’s a no-risk practice squad option. Taylor Decker, who refused a pay cut in Detroit and knows Ben Johnson’s offense, is the backup plan at left tackle with Braxton Jones done for the year, Theo Benedet playing through a shoulder injury, and Ozzy Trapilo not fully back. Joey Bosa, just 31 and coming off 29 of 34 games played over two seasons, appears soft retired and is only an option if injuries hit the edge. Will Dissly is the best true free agent tight end available, a good blocker who knows heavy personnel, though questions about his health and desire remain. And Jordan Elliott, cut by Tennessee after a firearm incident in the parking lot but strongly defended by Robert Saleh, worked out for the Bears and could land on the practice squad if the team is comfortable with his character.

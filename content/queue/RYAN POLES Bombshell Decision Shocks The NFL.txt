RYAN POLES BOMBSHELL DECISION SHOCKS THE NFL

Case Keenum has quietly set the foundation for Chicago’s next big draft move. Ryan Poles is staring down Los Angeles’s stubborn refusal to move Khalil Mack, and pivoting toward Jadeveon Clowney in Houston for a bargain price. But all of that is just the opening act. Because there’s a much bigger blueprint circulating around the league right now, one that has the entire NFC nervous. A plan to pair Montez Sweat with the second-highest graded pass rusher on the planet, according to PFF. A 27-year-old game wrecker sitting on 10 sacks right now.

Mortgaging the future to grab Jeffery Simmons for two first-round picks told us everything about where the Chicago Bears stand right now. This is a team in win-now mode. No more waiting. No more “next year.” The window is open, and Ryan Poles is going for it.

But handing over all that draft capital also opened a second door. A door that could let Chicago win back some valuable draft picks before the trade window slams shut. And standing right in the bullseye of that opportunity is backup quarterback Tyson Bagent.

Here’s why. A division rival is in total meltdown, and that meltdown has pushed Bagent’s trade value about as high as it will ever get.

Let’s talk about the Minnesota Vikings. Carson Wentz got eaten alive on Monday Night Football against the Buffalo Bills on November 9th. It was ugly. Kyler Murray is hobbled with a hamstring pull. And J.J. McCarthy, the young quarterback the Vikings dumped for a fifth-round pick back in late September? He just went 2 and 0 as a starter for the New York Giants. You can’t make this stuff up.

Meanwhile, Minnesota has dropped two straight games while averaging just 10.5 points per game. Ten and a half points. That’s not an offense, that’s a crisis. And behind center, Minnesota has nobody left except rookie Max Brosmer, with zero outside help available before the deadline.

Minnesota has backed itself into a dark corner. And that’s exactly why Chicago holds all the leverage.

Let’s look at Bagent’s situation. He’s in year one of a two-year extension worth $10 million. His work across three appearances this year hasn’t lit the world on fire. Nobody’s mistaking him for a franchise quarterback. But circumstances in Minnesota are anything but normal, and their total lack of options has jacked up what Chicago can demand for him right now. A second-round pick is achievable. A third-round pick should be the absolute floor.

And here’s the cold truth. Chicago gets a stronger return by shipping Bagent out today than it ever will at a future deadline, once Minnesota’s panic cools off. Desperation doesn’t last forever. Leverage like this doesn’t come around often.

Look, Tyson Bagent is a fun story. The small-school kid who fought his way into the NFL. Everybody loves that. But at the end of the day, he’s still a backup quarterback. And if Minnesota is desperate enough to hand Ryan Poles a day two pick because their season is going down the drain, you pack Bagent’s bags and drive him to O’Hare yourself.

Now, I know what some of you are thinking. Isn’t it risky to trade away a backup quarterback while Caleb Williams is running the offense? It’s a fair concern. Williams has already missed games this year, after that Week 2 hamstring strain against Minnesota. That’s been his only real medical bump in the road so far. But injuries happen, and you always want a plan behind your franchise quarterback.

And that’s where the good news comes in. Chicago already has that plan. Veteran quarterback Case Keenum is sitting right there in the quarterback room. And when Keenum was called upon in Week 3 against the Philadelphia Eagles, he delivered one of the cleanest backup performances Chicago has seen in years. Two hundred forty-seven passing yards. Two touchdowns. And a 27-point effort in a statement win over Philadelphia.

That performance changed everything. Case Keenum coming in and putting up 27 points on the Eagles made Tyson Bagent expendable on the depth chart overnight. Chicago doesn’t need two backup quarterbacks when one savvy veteran like Keenum can step in and win games without turning the ball over. That’s why I said at the top that Keenum set the foundation for this move. Without that Eagles game, this conversation doesn’t happen.

And the draft capital matters. The Chicago Bears walk into this deadline holding a second and third-round pick in 2027, and then there’s a gap all the way until round five. After the Simmons trade, every pick counts. Adding a day two pick back into that pile would give Ryan Poles a lot more flexibility in the offseason.

This isn’t the first time Bagent’s name has come up, either. Chicago surfaced in Bagent trade chatter earlier this season. Sports Illustrated’s Jason La Canfora reported that the Baltimore Ravens reached out to Chicago about Bagent after Lamar Jackson and backup Tyler Huntley both suffered significant injuries. Baltimore ultimately went another direction and acquired Jacoby Brissett from Arizona. That move cleared the way for Gardner Minshew to start for the Cardinals.

But then Minshew went down in Week 9 with a torn UCL, and according to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, he’s likely out for the season. And that brings Arizona right back into the conversation. The Cardinals had been intrigued by Bagent well before this season even started. Local radio host John Gambadoro reported Arizona’s interest back in February. And Ryan Poles later confirmed publicly that trade interest in Bagent came in during the offseason.

So it’s not just Minnesota. There’s a market. Multiple teams have shown interest. And when there’s a market, the price goes up.

This front office isn’t emotional about backup quarterbacks. Chicago is building a championship roster. But I want to put this decision in your hands. If you were Ryan Poles, would you trade Tyson Bagent, or would you keep him? Drop “trade” or “keep” in the comments right now.

Because here’s the thing. Trading away a backup quarterback brings in draft picks. But draft picks don’t sack quarterbacks in January. To win a Super Bowl, Ryan Poles has to keep pouring assets into the defensive front. And that brings us straight to the edge rusher market.

Chicago’s interest in a potential reunion with Khalil Mack has been well documented around the league. Mack is still with the Los Angeles Chargers, a team that entered the trade deadline without a single win in eight games. Zero and eight. But despite that record, the Chargers have been remarkably consistent about one thing: they’ve refused to move their most valuable players, and Khalil Mack is included in that group.

So if the Chargers won’t budge, Poles needs a Plan B. And Jadeveon Clowney is exactly the target he needs to be calling right now.

Clowney was the first overall pick in the 2014 NFL Draft. Thirteen seasons later, he’s still going, and right now he’s finishing out a one-year, $5.5 million contract with the Houston Texans. The Texans are sitting at 3 and 8, and while they’re not guaranteed sellers, NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo reported, and Mike Florio at Pro Football Talk cited in a November 8th report, that Houston has already started fielding calls. And Clowney’s name was specifically identified as one generating interest around the league. That same report noted that Clowney would likely welcome a move to a contending team.

Think about that. A former number one overall pick, on a $5.5 million cap hit, who wants to play for a contender. That’s highway robbery for a player with that kind of pedigree. Bring him to Chicago, where the Bears are actually playing for something real.

In six appearances for Houston this season, Clowney has posted 3.5 sacks and six quarterback hits. Now, let’s be realistic. He’s not a full-time starter at this stage of his career. But as a situational edge rusher, supplementing what Chicago already has up front, he’d be a meaningful addition to any game plan.

And the price? CBS Sports analyst Jared Dubin projected Clowney’s trade value at roughly a 2027 sixth or seventh-round pick. A sixth-round pick for Jadeveon Clowney. Picture him rotating in on third down, fresh legs, giving opposing offensive tackles fits all night long. That’s a bargain.

Now, the money. ESPN’s Courtney Cronin laid out the financial reality after the Simmons trade. Chicago absorbed $7.9 million of Simmons’s remaining 2026 compensation, leaving Ryan Poles with just under $4 million in cap space. That’s a narrow margin. But Cronin specifically noted that bolstering the edge rusher group through a trade likely wouldn’t require more than a day three pick. And that lines up perfectly with what a Clowney deal would cost.

Now imagine it. Clowney’s pass-rushing ability on the edge, with Jeffery Simmons on the interior eating up double teams. That’s a defensive configuration most offenses simply can’t solve with sound blocking. Simmons collapses the middle, Clowney attacks from the outside, and quarterback pockets start falling apart like a house of cards. Make the call, Poles.

But now we get to the big one. Because a larger blockbuster idea has started circulating around the league, one that could completely redefine what this Chicago Bears defense can do. If Ryan Poles pulls this off, Chicago’s defensive transformation goes from impressive to an absolute nightmare for the rest of the NFL.

Remember a name that was the subject of so many rumors back in 2023? Chase Young.

Through 12 games with the New Orleans Saints, the 27-year-old edge rusher has recorded 10 sacks, 38 tackles, two forced fumbles, and three fumble recoveries. Ten sacks. He ranks third in the entire NFL in sacks, first in the NFC, and fourth in sack yardage with 48. And here’s the stat that should make every Bears fan sit up: among all edge defenders in football, only one player has a higher pass rush grade from Pro Football Focus than Chase Young. Only one.

And here’s the part that makes this fit so perfect. Montez Sweat and Chase Young played alongside each other in Washington for three and a half seasons, before both of them were traded ahead of the 2023 deadline. They know each other. They know each other’s games. They know how to run stunts together, how to set each other up, how to feed off each other’s pressure. That kind of chemistry was built over years of work. You can’t buy that on the open market. The second Chase Young walks into Halas Hall, Sweat and Young are ready to wreck NFC North offenses. Instantly. No learning curve.

And let’s be honest about why this matters. Ryan Poles and Ben Johnson chose not to select a single defensive lineman in the entire 2026 NFL Draft. Not one. The front office’s publicly stated approach was to trust improved health across the existing group and better coaching going forward. And look, there’s some logic to that. But hope isn’t a defensive scheme when you’re trying to win playoff games in January. Leaving a weak spot on the edge is basically begging good teams to expose you.

Now, is there real smoke here? The NFL Rumors account on X reported that both the Chicago Bears and the San Francisco 49ers reached out to New Orleans about Chase Young’s availability. And that account’s credibility carries real weight on this one, because it was the same source that reported Chicago’s contact with Tennessee about Jeffery Simmons several hours before that deal was finalized. When a source gets one big call right, you pay attention to the next one.

And Ryan Poles has had Chase Young on his radar for years, with reports of his interest tracing all the way back to 2023. This isn’t a random name. This is a player Poles has wanted for a long time.

So what would a deal look like? Young’s contract was recently restructured by New Orleans. If Saints general manager Mickey Loomis decides that stockpiling draft capital is the smarter long-term path, a deal becomes possible. A realistic offer might center on a 2026 or 2027 third-round pick, possibly with a veteran contract included to help balance the money. Young is under contract through 2027, which gives Chicago some useful flexibility. There is a catch, though: releasing Young after the 2026 season would create roughly $14.7 million in dead cap. So this isn’t a free move. It’s a calculated risk.

But think about the payoff. Chase Young next to Montez Sweat, with a defensive interior anchored by Jeffery Simmons. That’s a front that very few offenses in the NFL could consistently handle. And Ryan Poles has already shown us, with the Simmons trade, that price is not an obstacle when he believes the talent justifies the cost.

Now imagine being an offensive coordinator, staring at Montez Sweat, Jeffery Simmons, and Chase Young on the same defensive line. Who do you double team? You can’t double all three. You can’t even double two of them without leaving somebody else one-on-one. Someone is coming through clean on every single snap. That’s not just a good pass rush. That’s a nightmare. That’s the kind of front that wins games in January when the offense sputters, just like we saw against Tampa Bay.

So let’s recap. Ryan Poles has a golden opportunity to flip backup quarterback Tyson Bagent for a day two pick, with Minnesota’s quarterback room in shambles and Arizona’s interest back in play after Gardner Minshew’s injury, especially with Case Keenum already proving he can win games as the backup. With the Chargers refusing to move Khalil Mack, Jadeveon Clowney is the bargain alternative, a former number one overall pick on a $5.5 million deal who could cost as little as a sixth or seventh-round pick and fits within Chicago’s tight cap space. And the blockbuster dream is Chase Young, the 10-sack edge rusher with one of the best pass rush grades in football and years of chemistry with Montez Sweat, who could be available for around a third-round pick.

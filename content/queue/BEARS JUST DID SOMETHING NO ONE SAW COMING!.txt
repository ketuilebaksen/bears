The Chicago front office is making a quiet shift right now, and if you only stare at Sunday’s box score, you’re going to miss the real roster math happening upstairs. With Caleb Williams operating on a cost-controlled rookie contract for the next several seasons, before an eventual blockbuster extension hits the books, the Bears have real financial flexibility at the spots surrounding their franchise quarterback. This is the window. This is the time to lock up the right players at the right price before Caleb’s big payday changes everything.

Let’s start with the offensive line, and with a player who has completely turned his career around in Chicago. Jonah Jackson.

Jackson went from a career low point with the Los Angeles Rams to one of Chicago’s sharpest offensive line investments. When the Bears acquired him from Los Angeles in 2025, he’d missed the bulk of the previous season with injuries, and when he was on the field, his limited snaps hadn’t moved the needle. A lot of people looked at that trade and shrugged.

But Chicago’s coaching staff pulled a totally different football player out of Jonah Jackson this season. Through the first half of the year, Jackson ranks fourth among all NFL guards with an 85.1 run blocking grade, and fifth overall with an 83.1 grade. And here’s the stat that really jumps off the page: he’s surrendered zero sacks. Zero. With just 16 pressures allowed. That’s elite protection for Caleb Williams.

And Jackson’s contract situation makes a long-term extension the cleanest move on Ryan Poles’s board. He’s got just one year remaining after this season, with zero guaranteed money left. That gives Chicago complete control over how a new deal gets structured. And extending him now creates an immediate cap benefit. It would reduce his scheduled $12.2 million cap figure for 2027 and free up extra cash for other roster needs. You get a better player locked in longer, and you save money next year. That’s a win-win.

Next up, a player who forced his way into Chicago’s long-term plans: linebacker D’Marco Jackson.

Jackson arrived as an unexpected contributor, and he’s turned into something much more. His share of Chicago’s defensive snaps climbed from 26 percent last season to 43 percent this year. And his pass coverage has jumped right off the tape. He holds a PFF coverage grade of 76.2, which ranks sixth among all NFL linebackers. Sixth. He’s also picked off a pass this season. And his range in coverage makes him a perfect complement alongside Devin Bush, who’s been all over the field himself.

Now, to be fair, his work against the run has been uneven. Nobody’s saying he’s a finished product. But D’Marco Jackson’s expiring contract puts Chicago on the clock. He’s got one year remaining on his current deal. And with T.J. Edwards getting older in football years, the Bears would be smart to lock Jackson into a multi-year commitment before he gets anywhere close to free agency. Because if you wait until after another full season of top-six coverage production, his price is only going up. More guaranteed money. More competition from other teams. The time to pay him is now, before everybody else realizes how good he’s become.

Then there’s Cam Lewis, who has quietly become one of Chicago’s highest-value defensive additions during a year of non-stop injuries in the secondary. Think about how many times this season the Bears have had to shuffle the back end. Lewis has been the glue. He’s split his snaps between slot cornerback and safety, and he’s handled both assignments without flinching. He’s earned a 70.2 coverage grade, which ranks 17th among all safeties.

His current contract doesn’t expire until after 2027. But Chicago’s front office would be smart to pursue an extension early, because his production this year far exceeds what he’s being paid on his current deal. Players like Cam Lewis are exactly how great teams stay great. Locking down those under-the-radar bargains keeps the foundation strong while the big contracts start piling up.

But smart contract extensions only tell half the story of how this front office operates. Ryan Poles’s track record on the trade market since becoming general manager in 2022 explains how this roster really got built. Let’s walk through it, because the full ledger shows both the wins and the costs.

We’ll start with the one that stole every headline. Jeffery Simmons. And within his first two games in navy and orange, it was obvious the front office hadn’t overpaid. But let’s break down exactly what it took. Chicago sent its 2027 and 2028 first-round picks, plus a 2027 fourth-round pick, to Tennessee for Simmons and a 2028 fifth-rounder.

And this deal didn’t just happen out of nowhere. It required years of front office trust. Ryan Poles’s working relationship with Titans general manager Mike Borgonzi dates back to their days together in Kansas City. That rapport, combined with Poles putting two first-round picks on the table right from the start, is what closed this midseason blockbuster. Tennessee collected draft capital and cap space. Chicago got immediate, game-changing interior disruption. And based on what we saw against Tampa Bay, with Simmons living in the backfield, that trade is already paying off.

But now let’s go back to the trades that didn’t make as many headlines, because some of them have aged beautifully.

Khalil Herbert’s trade to the Cincinnati Bengals for a 2025 seventh-round pick looks like a minor footnote. Until you trace what that pick became. Chicago drafted Herbert in the sixth round back in 2021. He flashed real burst across his first three seasons, but his role vanished in year four. He carried the ball just eight times over the first six weeks of that season before Poles shipped him to Cincinnati ahead of the trade deadline.

And what did Chicago do with that seventh-round pick? They selected running back Kyle Monangai. One of the sharpest value picks in the entire 2025 draft class. A seventh-round pick who became one of the toughest, most physical runners on this team. That’s how you build a roster. Turning a player whose role had disappeared into a future contributor.

Then there’s DJ Moore. And this one might be Ryan Poles’s sharpest cap escape. Chicago traded Moore and a 2026 sixth-round pick to the Buffalo Bills for a 2026 second-round pick. Now think about the context. Moore was coming off a career-low 682 receiving yards the season before. Very few people around the league expected Chicago to get back a second-round pick. And even fewer expected Buffalo to absorb Moore’s entire four-year, $110 million contract.

But Poles pulled it off. And he didn’t stop there. Chicago then moved that second-round pick down in the draft and turned it into more value, landing tight end Sam Roush and helping fund the trade up to secure cornerback Malik Muhammad. So one veteran receiver coming off a down year turned into a young tight end, a rookie cornerback who’s already been flashing in this defense, and a massive chunk of cap space. That’s elite roster management.

And then, the toughest one of all. Khalil Mack.

Mack’s trade to the Los Angeles Chargers for a 2022 second-round pick and a 2023 sixth-round pick was Ryan Poles’s toughest financial reset. Poles inherited a severe salary cap mess left behind by former general manager Ryan Pace. And moving Mack’s massive contract off the books was the fastest way to create real breathing room during a full teardown.

Now, Mack piled up 25 sacks over his next two seasons in Los Angeles. That stings. Nobody’s going to pretend otherwise. But carrying that contract in Chicago would have suffocated the rebuild. And those picks didn’t go to waste. They eventually became Jaquan Brisker, Elijah Hicks, and Trenton Gill, after Chicago split that 2023 sixth-rounder into two 2022 seventh-rounders.

Now, here’s the catch with the Simmons blockbuster. Paying two first-round picks fixed the middle of the defensive line. But it also means Ryan Poles has to hunt in the bargain bin, without much draft capital, to patch up the back end of this defense. Simmons upgraded Chicago’s front seven overnight. But the cornerback room still lacks reliable depth behind the starters right now.

And that brings us to the news nobody saw coming.

On Saturday, November 14th, ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that the Pittsburgh Steelers had released cornerback Terell Smith. And the timing lines up perfectly with Chicago closing out trade deadline week without landing the secondary help it had been exploring.

Bears fans, you know this name. Terell Smith’s story in Chicago started when the Bears drafted him in the fifth round of the 2023 NFL Draft. He came in competing directly with second-round pick Tyrique Stevenson, who eventually settled in as the outside starter opposite Jaylon Johnson. And over his first two seasons with the Bears, Smith appeared in 26 games with six starts, posting 10 pass breakups, four tackles for loss, an interception, and a forced fumble. Not superstar numbers. But real, solid contributions from a fifth-round pick.

Then his career took a serious turn. During the 2025 preseason, Smith suffered a torn patellar tendon in his right knee. That’s one of the most difficult injuries a cornerback can go through. He missed all of Ben Johnson’s first season as head coach. And the lingering effects of that injury carried into the following year, which led to his release before the regular season. Chicago waived him at the end of August and didn’t bring him back to the practice squad.

From there, Smith bounced around. He joined the Washington Commanders’ practice squad on September 2nd. Then he moved to Pittsburgh in early October. And his lone appearance for the Steelers came on November 1st against the Cleveland Browns: 12 special teams snaps, zero defensive snaps. And now he’s been released again.

So why does any of this matter for Chicago? Because the need is real. Jaylon Johnson and Tyrique Stevenson have both had meaningful coverage struggles on the perimeter since August. Johnson did just have his best game of the season with that 67-yard pick six against Tampa, which is great. But Stevenson keeps going in and out of the lineup, and now he’s dealing with a hand injury. And injury-related absences have left Chicago’s cornerback depth stretched thin.

Adding Jeffery Simmons lifted the entire defense. But Chicago’s deadline pursuit of Eagles defensive back Riq Woolen, reportedly for around a fifth-round pick, never came together. So the hole at corner is still sitting there.

And that’s where Terell Smith comes in. With $4.2 million in career earnings, a reunion in Chicago would be a low-cost, zero-draft-pick move that protects the cap. Now, nobody’s claiming Smith has Riq Woolen’s résumé. He doesn’t. But he also doesn’t carry Woolen’s price tag, or a fifth-round draft cost. He’s 27 years old. He knows the building. He knows a lot of the people inside it. And for a Bears roster that needs immediate depth at cornerback, bringing a familiar face back on the practice squad is a practical, financially sound option.

Think about how this fits everything we talked about today. This front office has been at its best when it finds value where other teams aren’t looking. A seventh-round pick turned into Kyle Monangai. A struggling receiver turned into a second-round pick, a tight end, and a starting-caliber rookie corner. A cap-killing contract turned into three young players. Signing Terell Smith off the street for the minimum and seeing if he’s finally healthy? That’s exactly the kind of low-risk, high-reward move that fits the Ryan Poles playbook.

And here’s the honest caveat. The patellar tendon injury is a real concern. There’s a reason he only played special teams in Pittsburgh, and there’s a reason he’s been released twice in a few months. So no one should expect him to walk in and start. But as a depth piece, a practice squad body who can step up in an emergency, who already knows the culture in Chicago? It costs almost nothing to find out.

Let’s recap. With Caleb Williams still on his rookie deal, the Bears have a window to lock in key pieces before his massive extension arrives. Jonah Jackson has transformed into one of the NFL’s best guards, with zero sacks allowed and an 85.1 run blocking grade, and extending him now would lower his $12.2 million 2027 cap hit. D’Marco Jackson’s 76.2 coverage grade ranks sixth among linebackers, and with T.J. Edwards aging, he needs a new deal before his price explodes. Cam Lewis has been a versatile, underpaid gem in an injury-riddled secondary. Ryan Poles’s trade history shows a GM who turns tough decisions into long-term value, from Khalil Herbert into Kyle Monangai, to DJ Moore into Sam Roush and Malik Muhammad, to Khalil Mack into Jaquan Brisker, Elijah Hicks, and Trenton Gill, and finally, two first-round picks into Jeffery Simmons. And with no draft capital left to spare, a reunion with former Bears cornerback Terell Smith, just released by Pittsburgh, could be the zero-cost depth move this secondary needs.
